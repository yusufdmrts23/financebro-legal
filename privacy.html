When it starts. Advertising measurement is switched on by default. Meta's software starts the first time you open the App, so the install and first-open events are sent before you can change any setting, and the “finished setting up your profile” milestone — and a purchase, if you subscribe on the first offer screen — is sent before the Settings screen is available. Before that point the App does not mention Meta; the offer screen links to this Policy. From then on you can switch it off at any time.

How to switch it off. Go to Profile → Settings (the gear icon at the top of Profile) → Advertising measurement. In Portuguese the path reads Perfil → Ajustes → Publicidade → Medição de anúncios; in Spanish, Perfil → Ajustes → Publicidad → Medición de anuncios. Switching it off changes nothing else in the App or its price.

  • What stops, and when. The automatic install and app-open events stop at once, and from the next time you open the App, Meta's software is not started at all. Until the App is fully closed — swiped away in the app switcher, or ended by iOS; going back to it from the background does not count — events recorded in the few seconds before you switched it off may still be sent, and Meta's software may still contact Meta, with your IP address and device information: to fetch its own settings, to send its once-a-day tracking-permission status, and when you make a purchase (the purchase itself is not sent to Meta, although it can still raise the SKAdNetwork activity level described below).
  • It applies to one device. The switch is saved on your device, not in your account. If you use Financebro on more than one device (for example an iPhone and an iPad), switch it off on each one. If you delete the App and install it again, it starts switched on again, and the new installation sends its install and first-open events before you can reach the switch, and if you have an active subscription it may report it again as a restored purchase, with its Apple transaction identifiers — so switch it off again there.
  • If you switch it back on. A subscription you started while it was off, and that is still active, may be reported to Meta then, including the date you bought it.

Apple's SKAdNetwork. If Advertising measurement is on the first time you open the App, Meta's software registers the App with Apple's SKAdNetwork. If Apple attributes your install to a Meta advertisement, your iPhone itself — not the App — may later send Meta up to three short install reports over roughly the following six weeks, on a schedule Apple sets. Apple designs these reports to contain no identifier for you or your device: they say only that an install came from a particular Meta campaign and, where enough people are involved, a rough level of activity afterwards, such as whether a subscription followed. Switching Advertising measurement off stops the App updating that activity level, but it cannot cancel a report iOS has already scheduled. Version 2.5 (August 2026) also took part in SKAdNetwork on every device, including in Europe, but differently: the App itself registered the install with Apple once you finished setting up your profile, and reported whether you then finished a first lesson or subscribed.

Other controls. If you have a Facebook or Instagram account, Meta's own ad settings (in Meta's Accounts Center) let you choose whether Meta uses activity that businesses share with it to personalise the ads you see. That setting governs how Meta uses information, including events sent before you switched Advertising measurement off; it does not stop the App sending events. Because the App never asks for tracking permission, your iPhone's Tracking settings (Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking) and Apple's Personalized Ads setting are not a dependable way to stop these events. The dependable control is the switch in the App.

6. Data retention

We keep your data while your account is active. When you delete your account, your data is removed promptly from our live systems; residual copies in encrypted backups are purged within 30 days on a rolling schedule. Aggregated, non-identifying analytics may be retained.

One limit worth stating plainly: events already sent to a third party before you deleted your account or switched a setting off — to Meta for advertising measurement, or to our analytics and crash-reporting providers — are held under that provider's own retention rules and we cannot recall them. Deleting your account also does not yet remove the customer record our subscription provider, RevenueCat, keeps under your account identifier (your purchase history and, if an Apple Ads campaign led to your install, that campaign); email us and we will delete it.

Deleting your account from inside the App also switches Advertising measurement off on that device, so the App sends no further events to Meta from it (until the App is fully closed, Meta's software may still contact Meta as section 4 describes, and Apple's SKAdNetwork report may already be scheduled). Our analytics and crash reporting are not switched off: if you keep using the App afterwards, you do so as a new user, and analytics events are sent under a new identifier that is not linked to the deleted account. If you delete your account by email, or the App is still installed on another device, switch Advertising measurement off there, or remove the App, to stop further events.

8. Analytics & identifiers

We use first-party analytics (PostHog) and crash reporting (Sentry) that rely on device and usage identifiers to measure how the App is used and to fix problems. Those two are used for our own product purposes only — never for advertising.

Separately, since version 2.6, Meta's measurement software sends the install, app-open and purchase events and the three one-time milestones described in section 2, while Advertising measurement is switched on, so we can tell whether our advertisements worked. It creates its own identifier for this App on your device. It is not given Apple's advertising identifier (IDFA): we do not present an App Tracking Transparency prompt, and we switch off the identifier collection in Meta's software as well, so there are two independent reasons it never leaves your device. The events are sent from your device one by one, each with that identifier, your IP address and basic device information; Meta reports results back to us only in aggregate. We do not give Meta the identifier that is normally used to join activity across companies' apps. Meta still receives your IP address, device details and, for purchases, Apple's transaction identifiers, so what it may do with these events is governed by its own terms and Apple's rules, not by anything that makes linking them technically impossible.

Apple's App Store privacy label for this App declares no tracking. We made that declaration because the App never asks for tracking permission, never gives Meta Apple's advertising identifier or any account details, and we do not ourselves link data from the App with other companies' data. Apple's definition of tracking also covers a third-party SDK that combines an app's data with data from other companies' apps for advertising, even if the app's developer does not use it that way; section 4 describes what Meta's terms allow it to do with these events. The label lists the data these events contain — a device identifier, product interaction and purchase history — under the purpose Third-Party Advertising.

Apple Ads. If you found Financebro through an ad in the App Store itself, Apple's attribution service (AdServices) can tell us so. On every install, in every region, the software from our subscription provider RevenueCat that is built into the App asks Apple, on your device, for an attribution token, and sends it to RevenueCat together with your account identifier. RevenueCat then asks Apple which Apple Ads campaign, ad group and keyword, if any, led to the install, and stores Apple's answer on your subscription record, so we can see which campaigns lead to subscriptions. No advertising identifier and no App Tracking Transparency prompt are involved, and nothing about this is shared with Meta.
